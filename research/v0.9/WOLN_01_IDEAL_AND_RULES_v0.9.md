# WOLN — Simplified English

**World Open Language for the Internet and AI Era**  
**Draft:** v0.9 — language concept and working rules, not a ratified standard  
**Motto:** **Meaning First, Grammar Second.**  
**Keywords:** Simplified English · Flexible Grammar · Intuitive Speech · Open Vocabulary

> **How to use this document in a two-stage AI test:** First, give a different AI only `WOLN_02_BLIND_TEST_AND_SELF_SCORE_v0.9.md` and save its complete first response. **Then** give it this document. ## 1. What is WOLN?

WOLN is a proposed **open, simplified way to use English** for communication between people and with AI. It is **not** a replacement for standard English, nor a claim that English grammar is useless. It keeps familiar English words, spelling, much of English word order, and broadly English-like pronunciation; it makes many grammatical changes optional or unnecessary when meaning stays clear.

WOLN is inspired by:

- **English:** widespread learned vocabulary, familiar syntax, and internet/technical usage.
- **Chinese:** meaning and time words can carry information without changing the verb's form.
- **Spanish:** relatively regular sound–spelling relationships as a *possible guide* for future short-word pronunciation, **not** Spanish grammar.
- **Other languages, including Indonesian:** ways to express number or time without obligatorily changing each noun or verb.
- **Internet culture:** practical abbreviations and short forms already encountered online.
- **AI era:** language models often infer missing function words from context, but their understanding must be tested rather than assumed.

**Learn English. Use WOLN.** Full English is always accepted. A learner need not memorize a second full vocabulary to begin.

## 2. Principles and boundaries

1. **Meaning first.** Preserve agent, action, object, quantity, time, negation, conditions, and direction where relevant.
2. **English first.** Prefer recognizable English words and **English-like subject–verb–object order**.
3. **Simple by default.** Omit grammar that does not change the intended message.
4. **Flexible, not meaningless.** Allow understandable variants, but ask when several interpretations matter.
5. **Full English always compatible.** Never mark an ordinary English word invalid solely because a short form exists.
6. **Open vocabulary, natural evolution.** Users may propose abbreviations. Common use and comprehension evidence—not an AI guess alone—should decide recommendations.
7. **Written before spoken.** A text short form does **not** automatically have a settled short pronunciation.
8. **Practical safety.** In medicine, law, business orders, and safety instructions, prefer explicit detail over brevity.

## 3. Core grammar — working rules

| Topic | WOLN simple convention | Example | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic order | Usually English SVO | `I buy 2 app yday.` | Do not force Chinese word order. |
| Verb forms | Base verb preferred; no obligatory third-person `-s`, past `-ed`, or irregular past | `She work yday.` | Full English conjugations still understood. |
| Time | Use clear words / dates rather than verb tense | `We meet tmr 9:00.` | Without a time word, **do not invent** past/future. |
| Ongoing action | `-ing` is **optional**, useful to emphasize action in progress | `She working now.` / `She work now.` | `-ing` is not compulsory; ongoing meaning may depend on context. |
| Be verb | `am/is/are/was/were` may remain or be omitted where clear | `She happy yday.` | Do not assume a missing `be` changes the intended meaning. |
| Noun number | Base noun is acceptable with any explicit number | `1 app`, `2 app`, `3 child` | Keep inherent final `s` in words like `bus`, `glass`. |
| Articles | `a/an/the` optional | `Send file.` | Use `this/that`, names, or identifiers if reference matters. |
| Questions | English-like order plus `?`; no obligatory do/does/did | `You need info?` | Preserve whether something is a question. |
| Negation | Keep explicit `don't`, `not`, `no`, `can't` | `She don't need help.` | Never erase negation for brevity. |
| Modals | Keep meaningful differences: `can`, `must`, `should`, `may` | `We must stop.` | Ability ≠ requirement ≠ possibility. |
| Prepositions | `at/in/on` flexible for ordinary time and broad location | `We meet at Monday.` | **Inside** (`in`) and **on top** (`on`) differ when physical relations matter; `to/from` are not interchangeable. |
| Adjectives/adverbs | Prefer common English positions; `-ly` not obligatory | `She drive slow.` | Keep clarity when modifier scope matters. |
| Comparison | `more` / `most` acceptable; familiar `better`, `best` also accepted | `This app more fast.` | Prefer clear comparison target. |
| Conditions | Keep `if`, `but`, `because` when they change meaning | `If rain tmr, we stay home.` | Do not turn a condition into a fact. |
| Passive | Existing English passive accepted; shorter form possible | `File sent yday.` | If agent/completion is important, state it explicitly. |
| Commands | Short imperative verb phrase | `Send 2 file to Ana.` | Recipient and deadlines remain critical. |

### Contrast examples

| Standard English | WOLN simple | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| She was happy yesterday. | `She happy yday.` | She was happy yesterday. |
| She is working now. | `She working now.` / `She work now.` | She is working now (context may matter). |
| I bought two apps yesterday. | `I buy 2 app yday.` | I bought two apps yesterday. |
| We will meet tomorrow. | `We meet tmr.` | We will meet tomorrow. |
| Does she need an AI tool? | `She need AI tool?` | Does she need an AI tool? |
| The book is in the box, not on it. | `Book in box, not on box.` | Physical distinction remains important. |

## 4. Open short-word vocabulary

A **candidate** is *not* automatically an established global abbreviation. WOLN accepts full originals regardless of the candidate's status.

| Full English | Short form | Status / note |
|---|---|---|
| application | `app` | Widely used. |
| information | `info` | Widely used. |
| technology | `tech` | Widely used. |
| photograph | `photo` | Widely used. |
| tomorrow | `tmr` | Used in informal online writing; not universal. |
| yesterday | `yday` | Informal candidate; may need context. |
| message | `msg` | Common in writing; pronunciation is not settled by spelling. |
| please | `pls` | Common in informal writing. |
| people | `ppl` | Common in informal writing. |
| analyze | `alyz` | **Experimental WOLN proposal**, not established. |
| composition | `cmp` | **Experimental, ambiguous**: also resembles other words/abbreviations. |
| year to date | `YTD` | Established finance meaning; **do not silently map `ytd` to yesterday**. |

Preferred development goal for *new spoken short forms*: **1–4 syllables**, with recognition and sound-collision tests. Two or three clear syllables may be preferable to one highly ambiguous syllable. English words and familiar English pronunciations remain valid. **No standardized pronunciation for `tmr`, `yday`, `alyz`, or `cmp` has been validated by WOLN yet.**

## 5. How an AI should handle WOLN

- Interpret the **intended meaning** from ordinary English vocabulary and context; do not automatically correct the user's grammar unless asked.
- Expand familiar abbreviations when confident; **label uncertain expansions** as guesses.
- Keep quantities, dates, negation, directions, comparisons, and `if` conditions intact.
- Do not infer whether an undated action occurred yesterday, happens now, or will happen tomorrow.
- If an abbreviation such as `cmp` or `ytd` has multiple plausible readings, **say so** rather than pretending to know.
- Standard English input and WOLN input are both acceptable. When asked to output WOLN, prefer concise understandable English-like wording.
- Understanding a text abbreviation is different from a voice recognition system recognizing a novel pronunciation; do not claim speech support without tests.

## 6. Development and openness

WOLN is a proposal for an **open living vocabulary**, potentially hosted in public Markdown/GitHub, later usable in AI Skills, chat interfaces, and mobile apps. The language does not depend on GitHub or a Skill: a single Markdown specification can be enough to guide a capable text AI *within a conversation*. Long-term consistency, adoption, and spoken recognition need independent tests.

## Scoring protocol

- The blind test has **20 items × 5 semantic anchors = 100 points**.
- Award **1 point for each anchor accurately conveyed in the AI's *original* English paraphrase or explicit uncertainty note**. Award **0** if missing, contradicted, or expressed as an unjustified certainty. Equivalent natural paraphrases are acceptable.
- Some anchors specifically reward **not inventing facts** or **flagging ambiguity**. If the answer adds a conflicting invented detail, deduct that anchor even if the rest sounds fluent.
- Do **not** change the AI's original answers before scoring. Quote key errors and show per-item scores.
- The AI's initial self-rating is **confidence, not validated comprehension accuracy**. Final scores from this shared key are still self-grading; an independent human reviewer is preferable.
- Reanswering the *same* items after reading this key is **not** an unbiased post-learning test. Use fresh unseen items for that claim.
