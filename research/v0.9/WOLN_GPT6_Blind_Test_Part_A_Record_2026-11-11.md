## 4. Model comments and preliminary observations

These are preliminary observations from the blind attempt, not claims about the official WOLN rules.

- **The short messages are often understandable from context.** Many items use familiar abbreviations such as `tmr`, `yday`, and `msg`; the main sentence meaning can often be inferred without formal grammar correction.
- **Special vocabulary is the largest uncertainty.** Items 18 and 19 are difficult because `Alyz`, `cmp`, and `ytd` may follow a WOLN-specific convention that cannot safely be inferred from ordinary English alone.
- **Numbers should be preserved literally during a blind test.** In items 04, 08, 13, and 20, the number forms may carry deliberate meaning. The first response therefore kept the numbers rather than silently converting or removing them.
- **Tense and time need careful treatment.** Items 11 and 17 do not explicitly mark tense in the same way as Standard English. Context suggests possible readings, but the official specification may define a more precise interpretation.
- **Semantic understanding is different from grammar correction.** The task asks for the intended message, so the paraphrases prioritize meaning while retaining stated quantities, conditions, negatives, comparisons, and locations.
