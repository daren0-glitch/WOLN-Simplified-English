# WOLN — Simplified English

**World Open Language for the Internet and AI Era**  
**Context** · V1.0 Draft · Grammar baseline V0.9 Revision 2, partially approved  
**Motto** · Meaning First, Grammar Second.

WOLN is an open, English-first proposal for simpler communication among people and with AI. Its three pillars are Simplified English, Flexible Grammar and Intuitive Speech. It accepts full Standard English and familiar English pronunciation. The priorities are meaning, clarity, English familiarity, simplicity and shortness, in that order.

## Write and understand WOLN

1. Keep familiar English subject–verb–object order when applicable. Prefer short, clear sentences. In casual chat, omitting a recoverable subject is a proposed option.
2. Use the English base verb by default, as in `she work`, `I go yday` and `we meet tmr`. Use explicit time words or dates as needed. Standard English inflections and tense constructions remain compatible.
3. Keep or omit `am/is/are/was/were` where the meaning stays clear. Optional `-ing` can emphasize ongoing action. `She working yday at 9.` refers to past time. `She working.` is usable when context supplies the time.
4. Use a base noun with an exact count, such as `1 app`, `2 app` or `3 child`. Keep letters that belong to the base word, such as the `s` in `bus`. An unnumbered base noun may leave quantity open.
5. Articles `a/an/the` are optional. Use `this`, `that`, a name or another explicit reference where identity matters.
6. Preserve question words and intent. Short forms such as `You like coffee?` illustrate a proposed writing pattern. Standard English question forms are accepted.
7. Use `don't` + base verb for ordinary simplified negation. Preserve meanings expressed by `no`, `not`, `can't` and modal verbs. Keep `if` where an outcome depends on a condition.
8. Allow flexible `at/in/on` when the intended time or location stays clear. Preserve distinctions such as `in box` and `on box`, `to` and `from`, and `turn on` and `turn off`.
9. Keep familiar infinitives, passive voice, comparisons, superlatives, possession and complex sentences as useful. Preserve the actor, action, object, recipient, quantity, time, aspect, condition, negation and direction where they matter.

## Vocabulary and ambiguous terms

Full English words are always usable. Prefer familiar English words, recognizable Internet forms and established short forms. Examples include `tmr` (tomorrow), `yday` (yesterday), `info` (information), `app` (software application), `tech` (technology) and `msg` (message).

Vocabulary is open. Forms can move through **PROPOSED → CANDIDATE → COMMON → RECOMMENDED** as evidence supports them. Existing English usage and WOLN review status are distinct. Treat `CMPSTN` (proposed for composition) and `ALYZ` (proposed for analyze) as experimental. Use `compare` when you mean compare. `cmp` can suggest compare, comparison, composition or another context-specific meaning. `YTD` can mean year to date. Use `yday` or `yesterday` for yesterday.

When interpreting input, preserve exactly what is stated. If multiple readings affect the message, keep the original term, give plausible meanings with uncertainty and ask a short clarifying question when needed. Avoid adding an unstated actor, recipient, time, completion or quantity. When writing for an unfamiliar reader, expand ambiguous short forms. A written abbreviation can always be read using the full English word. Short spoken readings remain research proposals.

| --- | --- |

| We will meet tomorrow. | `We meet tmr.` |
