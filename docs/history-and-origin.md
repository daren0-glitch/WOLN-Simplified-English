# History and origins

WOLN grew from a practical observation about international conversations among people who learned English as a second or additional language. Familiar words, clear intent and context can carry a message even when speakers use different grammatical forms. Text messaging adds a tradition of short writing. Language models provide another audience whose interpretation of that writing can be measured.

Esperanto, first published by L. L. Zamenhof in 1887, showed how a deliberately designed auxiliary language could develop through use and community participation. Much of its vocabulary draws from European languages. WOLN takes inspiration from its regularity and community development and starts from the English many people already encounter in international communication, technology and online.

Other languages offer specific design examples.

- **Mandarin Chinese** uses time expressions and other cues without obligatory person or tense inflection on verbs.
- **Indonesian** can express time through adverbs, auxiliaries and context. It offers another example of how time and verb form can be handled separately.
- **Spanish** has relatively predictable spelling–sound relationships. That is an inspiration for investigating readable short forms, with the sound system of WOLN based on recognizable English.

The project first documents written usage and then studies shorter speech. Experiments with people and AI can show which familiar forms travel well across settings.

## 繁體中文補充

WOLN 從跨語言的英語交流、網路縮寫與 AI 文字理解出發。世界語提供規律設計與社群發展的歷史參考。華語、印尼語與西班牙語則分別提供時間表達與拼音規律的研究線索。
