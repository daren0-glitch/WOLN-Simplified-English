| **G28** | **Passive voice** | **Keep the English passive and allow shorter passive expressions. Prefer the clearest wording.** | **OK** |

### Time expressions

| Standard English | WOLN |
| --- | --- |
| I go to work every day. | `I go work every day.` |
| I went to work yesterday. | `I go work yday.` |
| I will go to work tomorrow. | `I go work tmr.` |
| She was happy yesterday. | `She happy yday.` |

### Optional use of -ing

| WOLN | Emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| `She work every day.` | Routine work |
| `She work now.` | Works now, possibly in progress |
| `She working now.` | Explicitly emphasizes work in progress |
| `She was working yday at 9.` | Familiar English progressive with an explicit time |
| `She working yday at 9.` | Simplified expression for work in progress at nine yesterday |

The speaker can choose whether to use `-ing`.

### Optional use of the passive

- Standard English · `The file was sent yesterday.`
- WOLN Simple · `File sent yday.`
- English Compatible · `File was sent yesterday.`
- Active WOLN, when John is known to be the sender · `John send file yday.`

Use fuller wording when the actor or completion status matters. Preserve those details when the source provides them.

## H. Grammar framework

### 1. WOLN Simple

Used mainly for teaching, everyday chat, online communication and AI prompts.

- `She happy yday.`
- `I buy 2 app.`
- `We meet tmr.`
- `She working now.`

### 2. WOLN Flexible

Users can keep or omit grammatical elements in line with their English proficiency, provided the meaning is clear.

- `She is happy now.`
- `She happy now.`
- `I go to work tomorrow.`
- `I go work tmr.`

### 3. English Compatible

Full standard English is accepted as input, so English users can use the forms they already know.

- `She was happy yesterday.`
- `I bought two apps yesterday.`
- `We will meet tomorrow.`

All three forms are within the same WOLN compatibility framework.

## I. Flexible grammar in the AI era

WOLN can draw on language models' ability to interpret context, infer omitted information and recognize language variants. The draft distinguishes three claims.

| Claim | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| AI can interpret | A model may be able to infer the meaning of a simplified sentence. |
| AI verified | Tests measure how often a specified model interprets the sentence correctly. |
| Human understood | Comprehension is tested with people who have different first languages. |

The goal is human comprehension and preservation of key information. Grammar choices follow this order.

1. Meaning
2. Clarity
3. English familiarity
4. Simplicity
5. Shortness

**Meaning First, Grammar Second.**
