# Shorthand research

**Status** · Research proposal

Teeline is the main historical reference for testing typed English short forms. It uses recognizable written patterns while omitting letters that readers may be able to recover. Gregg and Pitman offer additional examples of how shorthand can represent sounds and omit selected details. WOLN experiments use ordinary text characters.

## Candidate design

- Start with existing short forms where available.
- Test whether omitting vowels leaves a recognizable consonant pattern.
- Keep important word boundaries and preserve negative markers.
- Check collisions with other words, names and domain abbreviations.
- Compare recognition, reading effort and writing effort with the full word.
- Try uppercase for experimental consonant forms when it improves recognition.

`composition → CMPSTN` and `analyze → ALYZ` are experiments. Record both positive and negative results. `cmp` can be read as compare, comparison, composition or another domain term. Keep `compare` in ordinary writing. Preserve its meaning by expanding a short form when context leaves it ambiguous.

The [short-word test plan](../research/short-words-test-plan.md) defines how to measure ambiguity and compare with full words. A shorter spelling offers a separate research question from how someone would say it aloud.
