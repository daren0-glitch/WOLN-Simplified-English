# Spoken WOLN

**Status** · Research plan. Validation pending.

Start with familiar English sounds and accept full English pronunciation. Study shorter readings only after defining their intended written meanings. Target 1–4 syllables for new short readings. Prefer 2–3 when that helps distinguish similar forms. Familiar one-syllable words such as `app` remain usable.

Use predictable sound–spelling patterns as a research inspiration, including examples from Spanish. Keep sounds recognizable to English learners, avoid unnecessarily difficult clusters and test near-homophones across accents. Written abbreviations can be read as full English words. A consonant pattern such as `CMPSTN` needs separate spoken research.

For each proposed reading, record the written form, full English reference, IPA, syllable count, speaker accents, similar-sounding items and results. Include single-word and sentence contexts.

1. Record speakers with different first languages, with consent.
2. Ask independent listeners to transcribe and explain the meaning. Compare the result with full-English controls. Report recognition, meaning preservation and confusion pairs by accent and context.
3. Run separate automatic speech recognition (ASR) trials. Record the engine and version, language setting, audio conditions and transcription errors.
4. Review the results before recommending a form. A recognizable written form can remain useful while its spoken equivalent is under study.
